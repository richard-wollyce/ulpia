First section

Body text, at the measure the book already uses. A paragraph says one thing, and a number in it carries its platform, corpus, commit and date in the same sentence. The library comes first and the term second: a note is a book, its Search for line is the label, the router is the catalogue, Vesta is the librarian, and a miss says I do not have that book.

  • A list item, when the items are parallel and short.
  • A second item. Two are fine; a list is not a rhythm device.

A smaller heading

Inline code such as kb route and a path such as fleet/yaron/knowledge/creatina.md sit on the plate. A key on the keyboard wears an edge: press Ctrl + C, with the plus in running text. There is no fourth heading level; a page that needs one is two pages.

A command and its output

A plate quotes the machine. The prompt and the output are one step down, the command is in plate ink, and the one line the reader must see wears class="agree", the terminal's own role. Code is written to 56 columns; a longer line scrolls inside the plate and the caption says so. Every plate with real output names its date and commit in the caption. Add proof to the class when the plate is the five-second proof beside a claim about an absence; it changes nothing visually and everything about what the section is allowed to claim.

A table

A ledger is data set in the book's own material, one step down in size. The wrapper scrolls on a phone and the page never does; it carries tabindex="0" so a keyboard reaches the right-hand columns. Numeric cells and their headers wear class="num". A table of two columns adds ledger-fit to the figure and drops the minimum width; three or more columns keep it and scroll.

A note in the margin

One component, two lead words, one colour. A note is ours, in ink, and the lead word does the work colour is not allowed to do.

Note. Something the reader should know before the next paragraph, in one or two sentences.

Careful. Something that costs the reader if missed. The same box; the word is the difference.

The words

A glossary is a definition list, stacked, the term in the 600 weight and the definition in running text. A term with two spellings is two terms on one definition. The concepts page owns the full list; a page defines only the words it introduces.

The line a person writes at the top of a note saying what questions it answers. The catalogue reads it; a note without one scores zero.
